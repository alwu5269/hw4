# Design: Campus Customs storefront

Goal: make the site feel like a real, friendly Yale shop, so shoppers enjoy browsing, trust what they see, and find their way to "Add to Cart".

## Fonts

- **Changed:** Libre Baskerville (a classic serif) for headings and the logo, Inter for body text, and small uppercase, letter-spaced labels for nav links, buttons, and categories.
- **Why it helps:** the serif headings read as "collegiate and established", like a Yale storefront, which builds trust; the clean body font keeps descriptions and prices easy to scan.

## Color

- **Changed:** Yale blue (`#00356b`) for the header bar, buttons, prices, and accents, with a warm cream page background, tan accents (string, bones), and a soft pink "Almost Gone!" tag.
- **Why it helps:** school colors make shoppers feel at home and proud to buy; one strong accent color makes every button and price easy to spot, and the pink tag adds gentle urgency on low-stock items.

## Hierarchy

- **Changed:** a clear top-to-bottom order on every page: announcement bar → header and nav → pennant garland → page heading → content. Product pages put the large photo on the left and name → price → description → sizes → stock → Add to Cart on the right. Chat search results appear in a highlighted "From your chat with Dan" box above the full catalogue.
- **Why it helps:** shoppers always know where they are and what to look at next, and the product page walks them straight from "what is it" to "is my size in stock" to "Add to Cart".

## Motion

- **Changed:** pages fade and rise in; garland flags flutter on hover; product cards lift on hover; the home-page bulldog gently bobs; bones wiggle when clicked and Dan grabs one and wags his tail; Add to Cart bursts into confetti. All motion turns off for people who set "reduce motion".
- **Why it helps:** small, quick animations make the site feel alive and responsive, reward clicking around (so people browse longer), and make adding to the cart feel like a little celebration.

## Product presentation

- **Changed:** rounded white cards with soft shadows, a pill-shaped price, a two-line description, stock count, and "Sold Out" / "Almost Gone!" badges; 20 products per page with page buttons; a two-column product page with size buttons (sold-out sizes crossed out and disabled) and a stock table. Add to Cart stays disabled until a size is picked.
- **Why it helps:** shoppers can compare products at a glance, trust the stock information, and never pick a size that isn't available, which means fewer frustrating dead ends before buying.

## Chat feel

- **Changed:** "Ask Dan", a bulldog-avatar chat with rounded speech bubbles, a bouncing tennis-ball "thinking" indicator, clickable starter questions, small product cards inside replies, and a message box that grows as you type. Search answers fill the page with matching products.
- **Why it helps:** the chat feels like talking to a friendly shop assistant rather than a form, starter questions show shoppers what to ask, and every recommendation is one click away from its product page.

## Imaginative touches

- **Changed:** original Handsome Dan illustrations (logo, chat, hero, a sitting Dan in the corner); a draped pennant garland with "Y" flags; a clickable bone pile; **"Put it on Dan"**, which dresses Dan in the product you're viewing (he swaps his collar for a bowtie in the School of Management shirt); little bone separators in the taglines; and on phones, Dan peeks in from the edge instead of covering the page.
- **Why it helps:** these surprises give the store a personality shoppers remember and want to share, and dressing Dan in a product is a playful way to picture it, keeping people on product pages longer, right next to the Add to Cart button.
